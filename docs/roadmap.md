# Research and adoption milestones

Success means other researchers reproduce a useful measurement and trust its interpretation. A name, star count or ambitious specification does not establish that.

| Milestone | Required evidence | Status in v0.2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portable evaluation | Package, contracts, licensed fixtures, reports, replay and CI | Implemented |
| External baselines | Three independently developed models on identical grounded tasks, with strong controls | Seeking submissions; no model scores published |
| Scientific validity | Disjoint worlds, metric calibration, power analysis, shortcut studies, human/physical checks | Research work required |
| External reproducibility | Two unaffiliated teams reproduce/submit studies without private assistance | Adoption target, not claimed |
| Prospective forecasts | Isolated execution, anchored pre-reveal commitments, failures, explicit feedback and independent outcomes | Research protocol; not the offline runner |
| Shared stewardship | Consenting independent reviewers and conflict handling | Sole maintainer; public governance |
| Research paper | Diverse measured models, ablations, validity and external replication | Write claims after evidence supports them |

Start an external campaign with one well-grounded task and a feasible compute budget. Expand after the measurement distinguishes meaningful capabilities and reproduces across systems. Combining unrelated scores does not create a universal ranking.

Research priorities: longer revisits, intervention preservation, prospective prediction and downstream transfer from generated training data. Every new metric/task needs a versioned definition and a boundary with earlier results.
